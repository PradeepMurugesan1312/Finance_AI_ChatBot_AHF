# Tax

## Choosing a tax code

The tax code on a purchase or sales document drives the tax rate and the tax
G/L account. Pick it from the tax code list by transaction type and
jurisdiction — for example domestic standard-rated purchase, domestic
zero-rated, EU reverse charge, import, or non-taxable. If none clearly fits,
route the document to the Tax team rather than guessing; a wrong tax code
misstates the return.

## Input vs output tax

Input tax (VAT / sales tax on what the company buys) is recoverable where the
purchase is for taxable business activity. Output tax (on what the company
sells) is collected and remitted. Both post automatically from the tax code to
dedicated tax accounts and are cleared when the return is filed.

## Filing calendar

Indirect tax returns are prepared by the Tax team and filed monthly, due by the
20th of the following month. The tax accounts are reconciled to the return
before filing. A late or amended return needs Tax Manager approval.

## Withholding tax

Payments to certain vendors — typically foreign vendors and some independent
contractors — are subject to withholding. The withholding tax type and code
must be on the vendor master before the first invoice. The company remits the
withheld amount to the authority and issues the vendor a withholding
certificate.

## Exemption and residency certificates

Customer tax exemption certificates and vendor tax residency certificates are
stored against the master record with an expiry date. An expired certificate is
treated as no certificate — standard tax applies until a valid one is on file.
